Every account covering an industry's news is running one of two strategies, whether or not it's been named: **reactive** (respond to something the moment it happens) or **curated** (round up what mattered over a period, on your own schedule). They're not competing approaches to the same goal — they solve different problems, on different timelines, and the automation for each looks meaningfully different.

## Reactive: speed is the entire value

Reactive content's value is almost entirely in timing — the same take posted within the hour of a story breaking versus posted the next day are different products, even if the words are identical. This is why reactive content automation leans hardest on tight polling intervals, relevance-and-recency filtering, and — because speed and hasty mistakes are close cousins — a review step that stays firmly human. (The full pattern: [automating newsjacking](/blog/automate-newsjacking).)

**Reactive fits your account if:** your audience follows your niche closely enough to notice timing, being visibly first (or fast) is part of your brand's identity, and you have the review bandwidth to check drafts promptly rather than let them sit for hours.

## Curated: consistency is the entire value

Curated content's value is the opposite — reliability, not speed. A "this week in [industry]" digest doesn't need to beat anyone to a story; it needs to show up every week, cover what actually mattered, and save your audience the work of following everything themselves. This is content that tolerates automation running further ahead without review, because there's no timing pressure making a delayed check-in costly.

**Curated fits your account if:** your audience wants a synthesized summary more than raw speed, you want a content calendar that doesn't depend on news actually happening on schedule, or you're building toward a format (a newsletter-style recap) that benefits from consistency over immediacy.

## The automation difference in practice

| | Reactive | Curated |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger | Frequent timer (15-60 min) | Weekly, fixed day/time |
| Filter | Recency + relevance, strict | Relevance + "notable," looser |
| AI step | One-line take, fast | Multi-item synthesis, more room |
| Review urgency | High — check promptly | Low — can sit for hours |
| Failure mode if unchecked | A wrong or dated take goes out | A weak week's digest goes out late |

Notice the review-urgency row: it's the main reason a curated workflow can trust a timer more readily than a reactive one can. Nothing about curation is time-sensitive, so a slower human check doesn't cost you the thing that makes the content valuable, the way it would with reactive content.

## Running both without them colliding

The two strategies don't compete for the same schedule slot, which is why running both is often the strongest setup rather than a compromise: a weekly curated digest as your reliable backbone (build once, mostly trust the timer), with reactive posts layered in whenever something genuinely big happens (built the same way, kept on a shorter leash). Neither needs to know the other exists — they're separate workflows, on separate triggers, feeding the same account.

## Choosing where to start

If you're building your first news-content automation, curated is the gentler entry point: lower stakes per run, easier to trust a timer sooner, and a format audiences reliably value even from a brand-new account. Reactive content pays off faster once you have it working, but it also punishes a rough first version more visibly — a stale or tone-deaf reactive post is more embarrassing than a merely mediocre weekly recap. Build the muscle on curation first if you're unsure; add reactive once you trust your filters and your prompt.
