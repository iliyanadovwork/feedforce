Every blurry, awkwardly-cropped carousel on Instagram is a sizing mistake that took two seconds to make and cost the post its polish. The rules are simple but unforgiving — Instagram silently crops or compresses anything that doesn't match its supported ratios. Here are the exact numbers, the safe zones, and the workflow that makes sizing a non-decision.

## The one answer: 1080×1350

If you take one number from this page: **make every carousel slide 1080×1350 pixels — 4:5 portrait.**

Why this beats the alternatives:

- **It's the tallest feed format.** A 4:5 post fills ~25% more screen than a 1:1 square and ~78% more than 1.91:1 landscape. In a feed, screen area *is* attention.
- **Every slide inherits slide 1's ratio anyway.** Instagram locks the carousel to the first slide's aspect ratio, so "mixing" isn't an option — you're choosing one ratio for the whole set. Choose the biggest.
- **It survives the grid crop gracefully.** Your profile grid shows a center crop; a 4:5 design with centered content previews cleanly.

Full reference table:

| Format | Pixels | Ratio | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Portrait** | **1080×1350** | **4:5** | **Use this** |
| Square | 1080×1080 | 1:1 | Fine, smaller presence |
| Landscape | 1080×566 | 1.91:1 | Avoid for carousels |
| Reels-size | 1080×1920 | 9:16 | Gets cropped to 4:5 in feed carousels — don't |

That last row is the most common self-inflicted wound: repurposing a 9:16 story/reel design into a carousel and losing the top and bottom of every slide.

## Safe zones inside the slide

Correct canvas size isn't enough — Instagram's UI overlays eat into your design:

- **Bottom ~180px:** caption, like/comment row overlap here in some surfaces. No critical text.
- **Top ~150px:** your username and the ⋯ menu.
- **Right edge:** the slide counter ("2/8") sits top-right.
- **Center-safe rule of thumb:** keep headlines and CTAs inside the middle 80% both directions, and nothing important within 60px of any edge.

## Design rules that ride on the sizing

1. **One idea per slide.** The swipe is a pacing mechanism; cramming defeats it.
2. **Slide 1 is a headline, not a summary** — its only job is stopping the scroll and earning the first swipe.
3. **Design the set as one system:** same background, type scale, and accent colors on every slide. Consistency is what makes a carousel feel authored rather than assembled.
4. **End with a CTA slide** — follow, save, or link. Saves weigh heavily in carousel distribution.
5. **Export without recompression:** if your tool offers quality settings, maximum quality PNG or high-quality JPEG at exact dimensions. Instagram will compress once; don't make it twice.
