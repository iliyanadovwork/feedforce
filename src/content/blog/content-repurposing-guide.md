The creators posting twelve times a week are not creating twelve times a week. Almost all of them run the same system: **one substantial piece of content, systematically shredded into platform-native derivatives.** It's called pillar-and-derivatives repurposing, and it's less a growth hack than an arithmetic trick — the thinking happens once, the distribution happens ten times. Here's the whole system.

## Pillars: make one thing worth shredding

A **pillar** is any piece of content substantial enough to contain multiple ideas:

- A 5–15 minute video (YouTube, webinar, podcast episode)
- A long-form article or newsletter issue
- A talk, livestream, or interview
- Even a dense 10-slide carousel can pillar for smaller derivatives

The quality bar matters more here than anywhere else in your content operation: derivatives inherit the pillar's substance. A thin pillar shreds into ten thin posts; a strong one into ten strong ones. Spend your creative energy at this layer.

## The 10 standard derivatives

From one video pillar (the richest kind), the standard extraction:

1. **2–4 vertical clips** — the strongest moments, subtitled, for Reels/TikTok/Shorts
2. **A carousel** — the pillar's structure as slides ([the video→carousel method](/blog/turn-video-into-carousel))
3. **A second carousel** — a different angle: the "mistakes" version of a "how-to" pillar
4. **2–3 quote cards** — the most screenshot-able lines in branded templates
5. **A text post / thread** — the core argument, native to X or LinkedIn
6. **A stat or chart post** — if the pillar contains one number that surprises
7. **A "one thing" post** — the single most useful takeaway, alone
8. **A newsletter section** — the pillar summarized with a link
9. **A community prompt** — the pillar's most debatable claim as a question
10. **The pillar itself, cross-posted** — full video to every long-form surface

Text pillars map the same way (pull quotes instead of clips). You won't use all ten every week — eight is a strong yield.

## The weekly cadence

The system runs on a one-week loop:

- **Day 1 — create the pillar.** The week's only heavy creative session.
- **Day 1, same sitting — extract while it's fresh.** Mark clip timestamps, pull quotes, list the derivative angles. Twenty minutes now beats two hours cold.
- **Day 2 — batch-produce derivatives.** With templates for each format (quote card, carousel, stat post), this is assembly, not design.
- **Days 2–7 — staggered publishing.** Pillar first, then derivatives spaced across the week, per-platform timing. Never everything at once: the point is *presence*, seven appearances from one effort.

Then the loop repeats. One pillar a week ≈ 35–45 posts a month.

## Automating the assembly line

Steps 2–4 of that cadence are mechanical, which makes them automatable. The pipeline version: the pillar's transcript goes in; a Custom Agent node reduces it to slide copy, quotes, and a text post in your voice; each lands in its branded template via an Apply Template node. There's no dedicated approval-gate node in FeedForce's canvas — the practical pattern is to run the workflow manually up through the template step, review the drafted slides in the editor, then publish once they look right, rather than wiring a timer trigger straight through to Post. ## The mistakes that break it

- **Deriving before the pillar is good.** Ten derivatives of nothing are nothing.
- **Same asset everywhere** instead of native re-expression — that's reposting, and each platform's audience can tell.
- **Publishing the batch simultaneously.** You get one loud day and six silent ones.
- **Skipping the system when busy.** The week you're too busy to repurpose is precisely the week the system exists for.

Repurposing isn't about squeezing more from less — it's the honest recognition that distribution, not creation, is where most content dies, and that your best idea deserves more than one attempt at finding its audience.
