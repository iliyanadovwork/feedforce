X is a text-first network that happens to carry video — which is exactly why a video pulled from it needs more rework than a repost between two vertical-video platforms. Resolution, aspect ratio, and caption all need a second pass before this content is ready for Instagram or TikTok.

## X video isn't shot for the destination you're reposting to

Two structural differences show up almost every time:

- **Resolution.** X compresses video harder than TikTok or Instagram do on upload, and it compresses again on download. A clip pulled from X and reposted has usually been through two lossy passes before a viewer ever sees it — visibly softer than the same clip posted natively would look.
- **Aspect ratio.** A meaningful share of X video is 16:9 landscape or square, because X's player handles those natively and a lot of what gets posted there is screen recordings, clips from broadcast, or desktop-recorded content — not vertical phone footage. Neither TikTok nor Instagram Reels wants that ratio; both expect 9:16.

Both problems compound: you're re-cropping a video that was already the lowest-quality version of itself.

## Get the cleanest copy you can

In order of preference:

1. **Your original file**, if you made the video yourself and still have the source export. No compression loss, no cropping decisions forced on you by someone else's framing.
2. **X's own video download**, where available — some accounts and clients expose a direct link to the underlying media file rather than the compressed feed version.
3. **A link-based downloader**, the fallback for a video you don't own the source for. Paste the post URL, get the video back without needing to screen-record it (screen-recording adds a third compression pass and often captures UI chrome along with the footage).

Whichever method, resist re-encoding more than once. Every extra export is another quality step down from a source that was already compressed going in.

## Fix the aspect ratio deliberately, not by default

If the source is 16:9 or square, don't auto-crop to fill 9:16 and call it done — decide what's actually in frame first:

- **Talking-head or single-subject video:** crop centered on the subject; the sides you lose are usually background.
- **Screen recordings or multi-element frames:** a straight crop can cut off exactly the part that made the clip worth reposting. Consider adding blurred-background padding above and below instead of cropping tighter, so nothing essential is lost.
- **Check safe zones per destination** after cropping — TikTok's UI sits differently on screen than Instagram's, so text or on-screen elements that clear one platform's overlay can land under the other's username or caption area.

## Rewrite the caption from scratch

A tweet's text is written to sit *next to* a video thumbnail, often assuming the reader already has context from the thread or the account. Move it over unedited and it reads like it's missing something, because it is. Instead:

- **State the point the video makes**, standalone — don't reference "this tweet" or thread context that doesn't exist on the new platform.
- **Drop tweet-specific phrasing** ("as I said above," "see thread") that only made sense in X's format.
- **Match the destination's caption culture**: TikTok rewards a natural-language, searchable line; Instagram Reels does well with something tighter plus a small hashtag set. Neither wants X's abbreviated, reply-guy tone transplanted whole.
